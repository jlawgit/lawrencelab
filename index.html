Polymers without the average.

We build polymers one molecule at a time, then use that precision to control structure: at surfaces, on nanoparticles, and in imaging agents.

Jimmy Lawrence Cain Department of Chemical Engineering

Approach

A conventional polymer sample is an average of many different molecules. That average hides the thing we want to measure, because any property you record is a property of the distribution rather than of a structure.

Working with molecularly uniform building blocks removes the averaging. Architecture becomes a variable we set rather than one we infer.

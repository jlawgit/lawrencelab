Final candidates

Three marks, three philosophies. Each is built from squares and circles on one grid, with one gap and one corner radius, so it can last thirty years.

Candidates

1 · Fit

Shape the part to its purpose.

The science
A building block shaped exactly to hold its function. The L is the precise block, the sphere is the functional unit, a particle or an active site. The equal gaps are the precision.
The principles
Integrity: the block is one whole piece. Mastery: a fit this exact takes skill. Honesty: two shapes and nothing else.
The name
Reads as L, as in Lawrence Lab.
Risks
Can read as a ball on a chair, which points to furniture. Needs a trademark search before use.
Colour
One colour
Dark

2 · Count

Every unit accounted for.

The science
Identical building blocks and one functional unit on an exact grid. The circle fits its cell, so the material is defined to the last part.
The principles
Precision and honesty: anyone can count every part. The gaps between the four cells form a plus sign, the grid that holds the parts in place.
The name
The three squares form an L.
Risks
Close to Google's Material widgets icon.
Colour
One colour
Dark
Outline
Negative space
32 and 16 px

3 · Three

Identical, equal, one.

The science
Three identical spheres in close packing, the smallest unit of an ordered material. Discrete building blocks that assemble without a defect.
The principles
Integrity as wholeness: three equal parts make one form. Monodisperse particles packing perfectly, with no part larger than another.
The name
None. The mark carries the idea, and the wordmark carries the name.
Risks
Three-circle motifs are common in molecule icons and some brands, so it is the least ownable of the three.

Before choosing: show each mark for five seconds to five colleagues and five people outside science, and ask what they see. Then search the US trademark database for similar designs and check LSU's rules for unit logos.

Explored: grid

Built only from squares and a circle on a strict grid: one gap (a sixth of a unit) and one corner radius (a seventh of a unit) everywhere, with the circle drawn 3.5% larger so it reads the same size as a square. Each mark is tested in colour, in one colour, on dark, and at 16 px.

Seated

One solid L-shaped block with a circle seated exactly in its corner, the same gap from both faces. A precisely shaped building block holding a functional unit, the way a ligand holds a particle. Reads as "L." at a glance. Unlike the two grids below, it does not look like an interface icon.

Three and one

Three identical squares and one circle on a 2 by 2 grid. The squares are building blocks, the circle is function (a particle, an active unit), and the circle fits its cell exactly, which is precision. The three squares also form an L. Simple enough to draw from memory, but close to Google's Material widgets icon.

Explored: J and L

JL · Tetris pieces

A J and an L, back to back, as true Tetris pieces. Reads as JL and as two blocks that fit. At this height it looks more like an upturned T than a peak.

32 and 16 px

JL · Tall

One block taller, so the stems rise like a single sharp peak above a flat baseline: a monodisperse MALDI spectrum. The two tones keep J and L readable. The last tile shows LSU purple and gold.

32 and 16 px

JL · One colour

The same shape in one colour. The seam becomes the centre line of the peak, so the spectrum reading comes first and the letters second.

32 and 16 px

JL · Blocks

Every block visible. Tetris and a bar spectrum at once, the most literal about building blocks. Busier at 16 px.

32 and 16 px
Explored: LL

1 · LL lock

Two identical L blocks lock into a 2 by 4 rectangle with nothing left over. It reads as LL, as building blocks, and as a perfect fit, and it keeps the tall column of the 2018 logo. The last tile shows it in LSU purple and gold.

32 and 16 px

2 · One block

A single L made of four identical units. The simplest form of the idea, like a letter set in blocks. Strongest at 16 px, but it says less than the lock.

32 and 16 px
